## The short version

At the moment, copying text on an iPhone and pasting it on a Windows PC is not something the two companies' own software does. Apple's **Universal Clipboard** is limited to Apple devices on the same Apple account. Microsoft's **Phone Link** supports an iPhone for calls, messages and notifications, not for the clipboard.

Reports published in 2026 say this is set to change. This guide summarises what has been reported. It is based on news coverage rather than Apple's own documents, so details may differ from what finally ships.

## What has been reported

- **A request from Microsoft.** Coverage says Microsoft asked Apple for persistent clipboard access for Windows, using the European Union's interoperability process under the Digital Markets Act. The request is reported as filed in **March 2026**.
- **Apple's agreement.** Apple is reported to have accepted a plan in **June 2026**. Headlines describe it as something Apple will do because the EU rules require it.
- **A long timeline.** Reports say the engineering work is expected to finish around **autumn 2027**, followed by developer betas and a public release, which would put availability at **late 2027 or early 2028**.
- **How it might work.** Coverage describes the PC being paired as an accessory, with the user approving sharing once, and iOS notifying an extension when something is copied so it can be sent to the PC.
- **Region.** Some outlets suggest the feature could end up limited to the EU. That is described as speculation, not as an Apple statement.

Treat the exact dates and mechanism as unconfirmed until Apple or Microsoft publishes them.

## Why it takes so long and needs a request

iOS is strict about the clipboard. Apps can generally read it only while they are in the foreground, and Apple added a notice that shows when an app reads it. That protects privacy, but it also means a Windows app cannot quietly receive what you copy. For this to work, Apple has to build a new, permissioned path and let other companies use it. The EU's interoperability rules are what pushed it onto Apple's schedule.

## What you can do until then

You need a middle step that both devices can reach. The simplest options:

1. **An online clipboard.** Paste on the iPhone, note the 6-digit code, open it on the PC. No sign-in and nothing to install. See [how to send a link from an iPhone to a PC](/guides/send-a-link-from-iphone-to-pc/).
2. **A message to yourself** in an app that also runs on Windows.
3. **Email or a notes app** for text you want to keep.

The guide to [iPhone to Windows without iCloud](/guides/iphone-to-windows-without-icloud/) compares them, and [what Phone Link can do with an iPhone](/guides/what-phone-link-can-do-with-iphone/) explains why Microsoft's app does not cover the clipboard.

## Should you wait for the built-in version?

A built-in feature will probably be convenient for people who always use an iPhone with a Windows PC. But waiting a year or more is not necessary, and a built-in sync may have limits. It may need Bluetooth pairing, work only between your own devices, or be tied to a region. An online clipboard has different strengths. It works on a computer that is not yours, it works between any two devices, including Android and Linux, and it does not need anything installed.

## What to watch for

- **Apple's and Microsoft's own announcements.** Look for iOS release notes and Windows Insider news rather than only third-party summaries.
- **Region.** Check whether the feature is available outside the EU when it arrives.
- **Privacy details.** A clipboard often holds passwords and private messages, so look at how permission is given and how data travels.
