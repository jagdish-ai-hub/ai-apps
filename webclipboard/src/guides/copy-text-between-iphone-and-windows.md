## Why copy and paste does not just work

Apple's "Universal Clipboard" lets you copy text on an iPhone and paste it on a Mac or iPad nearby, as long as they use the same Apple ID and have Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on. It is an Apple-to-Apple feature. A Windows PC is not part of that system, so the clipboard does not follow you across.

That leaves a gap for people who use an iPhone with a Windows laptop or a work PC. The fixes below all work by sending the text through something both devices can reach.

## iPhone to Windows with an online clipboard

This is the quickest route and needs no accounts.

1. On the iPhone, press and hold in a text field, choose **Select** or **Select All**, then **Copy**.
2. Open this site in Safari and tap into the text box on the **Send text** tab. Tap again and choose **Paste**.
3. Pick an expiry (10 minutes is enough) and tap **Save to clipboard**.
4. On the Windows PC, open the site, switch to **Receive text** and type the 6-digit code.
5. Click **Copy** to put the text on the PC's clipboard, then paste it where you need it with **Ctrl + V**.

Tip: tap the **Share link** button on the iPhone and send yourself the link by message. On the PC, opening the link brings the text up directly.

## Windows to iPhone

Reverse the steps. Copy on the PC with **Ctrl + C**, paste it into the **Send text** box, save it, and then either type the code on the iPhone or point the camera at the QR code on screen. Opening the link in Safari shows the text, and you can press and hold to copy it.

## Other ways to do it

- **Message or email yourself.** Paste on one device, open the same app on the other. Simple and keeps a record. See our guide to [sending text from a phone to a PC](/guides/send-text-from-phone-to-pc/) for a comparison.
- **Notes synced through a web service.** If you already use a notes service with a Windows app or website, paste into a note on the iPhone and open it on the PC. Be aware it stays in your account until you remove it.
- **Windows clipboard history.** Press **Windows + V** on the PC to see what you have copied recently. This works only on the PC, but it is handy once you have brought text across, because several pasted items stay available.

## Real-life situations

**Text inside a photo or screenshot.** Recent versions of iOS can recognise text in pictures: open the photo, press and hold on the words, and select them. Copy the selection, then send it to the PC through the clipboard. That turns a photographed page or a screenshot into text you can edit on the computer.

**A long address or reference number.** Instead of reading it out or typing it twice, paste it once on the iPhone and enter six digits on the PC. This is where mistakes disappear, because the characters never get retyped.

**A draft written in a notes app.** Write on the iPhone while you are out, then send the text to the PC and tidy it in a proper editor. If you plan to keep editing, move it into a synced document straight after.

**A verification code from a text message.** Codes like these are meant to be used by you only. Type them in directly rather than sending them through any transfer tool, and never share them with anyone who asks.

**Several items in a row.** Send each one as its own clip, or combine them into one clip with a line between each. On the PC, **Windows + V** keeps recent copies so you can paste them one by one.

## Making it faster

- **Add the site to your iPhone home screen.** In Safari, tap the share button and choose **Add to Home Screen**. It then opens like an app.
- **Use the QR code when both screens are in front of you.** Scanning avoids typing six digits.
- **Bookmark the site on the PC.** The page loads quickly and does not need a login.

## Keeping it private

Anyone who knows the 6-digit code while a clip exists can read it, so match the protection to the content:

- **Everyday text such as links and notes:** a short expiry is enough.
- **Anything private:** switch on **Delete after the first view** and add a password. The password encrypts the text on your device before it is uploaded.
- **Passwords and bank details:** do not send them through any quick-transfer service. Use a password manager.

## Troubleshooting

**The text pasted with strange line spacing.** Different apps handle line endings differently. Paste into a plain-text editor first if exact spacing matters.

**Safari will not let me paste.** Press and hold in the box until the **Paste** option appears, and allow it if iOS asks for permission.

**I closed the page and lost the code.** Codes are not stored with any account, so they cannot be recovered. Create a new clip, which takes a few seconds.
