## Why people want notes without an account

Most note apps start with a sign-up form. For a shopping list, a phone number you need for ten minutes, a few points for a call or a draft of a message, that is a lot of ceremony. You want a blank page, a way to make a list, and the ability to move the text somewhere else when you are done. An online notepad that works without an account gives you exactly that: you open a page and type.

There is also a privacy side. A notepad that keeps your text in your own browser does not need to know who you are, and nothing you write is sent anywhere while you type. For everyday notes this is often all you need.

## How a browser-based notepad works

When you type in a notepad like [this one](/online-notepad/), the text is saved in your browser's local storage, a small area every browser reserves for each website. The page saves your note a moment after you stop typing and again when you leave, so refreshing the tab or coming back tomorrow brings it back.

Because the note lives in that storage, three things follow. It is private by default, since it is not on a server. It is tied to one browser on one device, so you will not see it on your phone if you wrote it on your laptop. And it is only as permanent as the browser data: clear your cookies and site data, use a private window, or uninstall the browser, and the note goes with them.

## Formatting that does not get in the way

A good quick-notes page keeps formatting simple. Bold and italic for emphasis, a title, a heading to split a longer note, bullet and numbered lists and a checklist cover nearly everything people write in a note. The easiest way to support this, and to keep the note small and portable, is to store the note as plain text with light Markdown marks: two asterisks for bold, a hash for a heading, a dash for a bullet.

You do not have to learn the marks. A toolbar adds them when you select text and press a button, Ctrl+B and Ctrl+I work for bold and italic, and pressing Enter in a list continues the list. A preview button shows the note as it will look in a Word file or PDF. Read more about turning notes into documents in the guides on [saving notes as a PDF](/guides/how-to-save-notes-as-pdf/) and [converting notes to Word](/guides/how-to-convert-notes-to-word/).

## How to avoid losing a note

Browser storage is convenient but not a backup. Follow a few habits and you will rarely lose anything.

- **Download what matters.** A Word file, a PDF or a plain text file is a copy you control. Save it to a folder you back up.
- **Share it to move it.** To open a note on another device, share it with a 6-digit code. The other device types the code and sees the note formatted. See [how to share notes online](/guides/how-to-share-notes-online/).
- **Do not clear site data blindly.** Browser cleaning tools and some privacy settings delete the storage a notepad uses. If you clean your browser often, download your notes first.
- **Avoid private windows for notes you want to keep.** A private window forgets everything when it is closed.
- **Keep secrets out.** A note on a shared computer can be read by the next person who opens that browser. Do not keep passwords or bank details in a notepad.

## When an online notepad is not enough

An account-free notepad is the right tool for quick, short-lived text. It is not the right tool for notes you want on every device for years, notes you search through, or notes with images and attachments. For those, a note app with sync gives you what a browser page cannot: your notes on every device, version history and a backup you do not have to manage.

A sensible way to use both is to write quick notes in the notepad, and when something becomes worth keeping, download it or copy it into the app you use for long-term notes. The notepad is for the moment, the app is for the archive.

## A simple routine

Open the notepad, write, give the note a title if it matters, and press Preview if you want to see the structure. When you are finished, either leave it for later in the same browser, download it, or share it with a code if you need it on another device. That is the whole workflow, and it needs no account.
