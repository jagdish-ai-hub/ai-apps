## Copying and pasting is local

When you copy text, it goes to the **clipboard**, a small area of your device's memory. That copy does not travel over Wi-Fi and cannot be seen by other people on the network. The same is true when you paste it into a document on your own device.

What matters is the moment you **send** the text: pasting it into a login form, a chat or a website sends it across the network. So the question is not "is copy and paste safe?" but "is the connection I am sending it over safe?".

## What actually puts you at risk on public Wi-Fi

- **Fake hotspots.** An attacker can create a network with a believable name such as "Airport Free WiFi". If you join it, they control the connection. Always check the exact name with staff.
- **Unencrypted connections.** Almost all websites use **HTTPS**, which scrambles the data between your browser and the site so others on the network cannot read it. A page that does not, or shows a certificate warning, should not receive anything private.
- **Phishing pages.** A login page that looks right but sits at a slightly wrong address will happily collect what you paste. Check the address bar.
- **Captive portals that ask for too much.** A hotspot page may ask you to install an app, a certificate or a "security update". Do not.
- **Shoulder surfing.** In a crowded place, people can look at your screen while you type or paste.
- **Shared devices.** A public computer is a bigger risk than a public network; see the guide to [copying text to a public computer safely](/guides/copy-text-to-a-public-computer-safely/).

## How to stay safe

1. **Confirm the network name** with staff before joining.
2. **Use HTTPS sites** and look for the padlock. If your browser warns about a certificate, stop.
3. **Turn on two-step verification** on important accounts, so a stolen password is not enough.
4. **Use mobile data** for banking and anything highly sensitive. It is a private connection you control.
5. **Consider a VPN** from a reputable provider for added protection.
6. **Turn off automatic connection** to open networks, and turn off file sharing.
7. **Keep your device updated,** since updates close known holes.
8. **Log out** when you finish, and forget the network afterwards.

## Moving text between your own devices

Sometimes you need to get text from one device to another while you are on a public network: a code, a note, a link or a password. A few habits make that safe:

- Use an **encrypted, expiring clip**. In the [online clipboard](/), add a **password**; the text is encrypted in your browser before it is sent, so only someone with the password can read it.
- Choose a **short expiry** (10 minutes is enough) and switch on **delete after first view**.
- Share the **code** with your own device, not in a public chat. If someone else must receive it, give them the password by another route.
- Avoid pasting highly sensitive passwords into any site you do not trust; prefer your password manager's own autofill.

For guidance on passwords themselves, see [how to share a password safely](/guides/how-to-share-passwords-safely/).

## What the clipboard on your device can leak

Some apps can read the clipboard, and several operating systems show a notice when they do. If you copy a password from a password manager, clear the clipboard afterwards or let the manager clear it automatically after a short time. On a shared or public computer, never copy something sensitive and walk away, because the next person can paste it.

## Quick answer

- Copying and pasting on your own device is safe on any Wi-Fi.
- Sending text over public Wi-Fi is safe enough over HTTPS, but fake hotspots and phishing are the real dangers.
- For anything sensitive, use mobile data, two-step verification and short-lived, encrypted clips.
