## Why students run into this problem

A typical day looks like this: you jot down a reading list on your phone on the bus, you need it on the library computer, and later you want to continue the draft on your laptop. Each device has its own clipboard, and signing in to personal accounts on shared computers is something you should avoid.

An online clipboard fills that gap. You paste text on one device, get a code, and enter it on the other. It takes about ten seconds and leaves nothing logged in behind you.

## Seven ways students use it

### 1. Phone notes to a lab or library computer

Typed an outline in a notes app on your phone? Paste it into the **Send text** box, save it, and on the lab computer type the code on the **Receive text** tab. Copy the text into your document and close the browser. There is no account to sign out of.

### 2. Links and references

Found a useful article on your phone? Send the link to your laptop instead of emailing it to yourself. The same works for a list of sources you want to cite: paste the whole list, then paste it into your reference manager or bibliography on the PC.

### 3. Group project hand-offs

Send meeting times, a task list or a draft of the introduction to teammates as a clip with a 1-day expiry. Each teammate opens the link and copies what they need. When the group is ready to merge everyone's work, move it to a shared document that people can edit together.

### 4. Moving text out of a lab computer

If you wrote something on a school computer and your own laptop is not connected, paste it into a clip, set the expiry to **10 minutes**, and open the link on your own device. Remember to switch on **Delete after the first view** so the text does not stay around.

### 5. Online classes and video calls

Meeting links, passcodes and breakout-room instructions are awkward to type from a phone to a laptop. Send them as a clip and open the link on the other device.

### 6. Typing on a phone, editing on a computer

Some people prefer to dictate or type rough thoughts on a phone and tidy them up on a computer. Send the raw text across, edit it in a proper document, and keep that document in your normal cloud storage.

### 7. Sending yourself reminders for a shared computer

When you will be on a computer where you cannot install anything, paste what you will need into a clip the night before and set the expiry for one day.

## Good habits for shared computers

- **Use a private or incognito window** if the computer is shared, and close it when you finish.
- **Do not save passwords** when the browser offers.
- **Switch on delete after first view** for anything personal, and give it a short expiry.
- **Never send passwords for your accounts** through any quick-transfer tool. Use a password manager.
- **Do not leave a tab open** with the text on screen when you walk away.

## What it is not for

An online clipboard is temporary on purpose. It is not a backup for your coursework and it is not for submitting assignments. Keep your finished work in your normal cloud storage or school system, and use the clipboard to carry text between devices on the way there.

It is also not for anything that breaks your school's honesty rules. Study notes, drafts and your own references are fine. Exam answers are not.

## A quick checklist

1. Paste text on device A.
2. Choose a short expiry, and delete after the first view if it is private.
3. Save and read the 6-digit code, or scan the QR code.
4. Open the site on device B, enter the code, copy the text.
5. Close the tab. Done.
