## What iCloud for Windows does and does not do

Apple's iCloud for Windows connects a PC to your Apple account so that things like photos, files, passwords and bookmarks follow you around. It is useful for that. It is not a clipboard: text you copy on the iPhone does not appear on the PC, and nothing you copy on the PC lands on the iPhone. The Mac-style trick of copying on one device and pasting on another is called Universal Clipboard, and it works only between Apple devices that use the same Apple account.

So even if you install iCloud for Windows, you still need a way to move a piece of text. Often it is simpler to skip the install and use something lighter.

## Method 1: an online clipboard (no sign-in at all)

1. Open the [online clipboard](/) in Safari on the iPhone and paste the text into **Send text**.
2. Pick a short expiry such as **10 minutes** and tap **Save to clipboard**.
3. On the PC, open the site, choose **Receive text**, and type the 6-digit code. (Going the other way, from the PC to the iPhone, you can scan the QR code the PC shows with the iPhone camera instead.)
4. Copy the text and paste it where you need it.

It works on any browser on any PC, including computers you do not own, and nothing is stored in an account. It is the best choice for one-off transfers.

## Method 2: message yourself

Most chat apps, such as WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and LINE, have a way to send a message to yourself or a "saved messages" chat. Paste the text on the iPhone, then open the desktop or web version of the same app on the PC and copy it out. It is quick if you already use the app on the PC. The text stays in your chat history until you delete it, so avoid it for passwords.

## Method 3: email yourself

Email works anywhere and is good for long text. Paste the text into a new email to yourself, send it and open it on the PC. The drawbacks are speed and the need to sign in to your mailbox on the PC, which you may not want to do on a shared computer.

## Method 4: a notes app that works in a browser

Apps such as Google Keep, Microsoft OneNote and Notion have web versions, so notes you write on the iPhone can be opened in a PC browser after signing in. Apple's own Notes can also be read in a browser at iCloud.com if you sign in with your Apple account, which does not require installing iCloud for Windows. This is the best route for text you will keep editing, but it stores the text in an account and needs a sign-in on the PC.

## Method 5: the same browser on both

If you use Microsoft Edge or Chrome on both devices and are signed in to the same browser account, you can send a page from one device to the other or open recent tabs from the other device. It is good for continuing to read a page, less so for sending a block of text.

## Which one should you pick?

- **A work, school or public PC:** the online clipboard, because it needs no sign-in and leaves nothing behind.
- **Something you want to find again:** a message to yourself or a notes app.
- **A long document with attachments:** email.
- **You live in a browser on both devices:** the browser's own sync.

## Privacy checklist

Whatever you pick, think about where the text will stay:

- **Chat history and email** keep text for years. Delete sensitive items afterwards.
- **A clip** disappears when it expires or after the first view if you choose that option. Add a password to encrypt it in your browser.
- **A notes app** keeps everything in your account until you delete it.
- **A shared PC** can keep the clipboard of the browser in memory; clear it, or copy something harmless afterwards. The guide to [copying text to a public computer safely](/guides/copy-text-to-a-public-computer-safely/) covers this.
