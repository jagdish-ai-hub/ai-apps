## The quick way: a browser tool

The simplest method works the same on every device. Open the <a href="/text-to-pdf/">Text to PDF</a> tool, paste your text, choose a font, size, page size and margins, and press **Save as PDF**. Your browser opens its print window; choose **Save as PDF** as the destination and confirm.

Because the page prepares the PDF through the browser's own print function, three good things follow:

- **Nothing is uploaded.** The text stays on your device from start to finish.
- **Every language works.** The PDF uses the fonts already installed on your phone or computer, so Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Hindi, Thai, Arabic and Cyrillic text prints correctly.
- **No watermark, no account, no limit.** There is nothing to pay for because nothing runs on a server.

If you received the text on another device first, you can bring it across with the [online clipboard](/), then convert it where you need it. The guide on [sending text from a phone to a PC](/guides/send-text-from-phone-to-pc/) explains that step.

## On Windows without any website

Windows 10 and 11 include a printer called **Microsoft Print to PDF**. Open your text in Notepad, press **Ctrl + P**, pick that printer and press **Print**. Windows asks where to save the file. The result is plain: Notepad prints in one font and does not let you set margins in a friendly way, so for anything you will send to someone else a tool with font and margin choices gives a better-looking document.

If you use Word or Google Docs, paste the text there, tidy it up and use **File, Save as, PDF** in Word, or **File, Download, PDF document** in Google Docs. That is the best route when you also want to add a heading, a logo or a table of your own.

## On a Mac

Open the text in TextEdit (or paste it into any app), press **Command + P**, and use the **PDF** drop-down at the bottom left of the print window, then choose **Save as PDF**. In Safari you can also use **File, Export as PDF** for a web page. The browser tool above works in Safari, Chrome and Firefox on a Mac in the same way.

## On an iPhone or iPad

iOS has no "Save as PDF" button for plain text, but it has a trick that works in almost every app:

1. Open the print options (the share button, then **Print**).
2. In the print preview, **spread two fingers** over the small page thumbnail. It opens as a full-size PDF.
3. Tap the **share button** and choose **Save to Files** (or send it by mail or message).

Using the browser tool in Safari follows the same steps after you press **Save as PDF**.

## On Android

In Chrome, press the button on the tool page, then in the print window choose **Save as PDF** as the destination and tap the PDF button. Other apps have **Share, Print, Save as PDF** in their menus. The file lands in your Downloads folder unless you choose another place.

## Choosing the right settings

- **Font:** a serif font suits letters and long reading, sans-serif suits notes and lists, monospace keeps columns of text and code aligned.
- **Size:** 11 or 12 pt is standard. Use 10 pt to fit more on a page, or 14 to 16 pt for easy reading.
- **Margins:** normal margins (about 2.5 cm or 1 inch) look professional. Narrow margins fit more text, and wide margins leave room for handwritten notes.
- **Page size:** A4 or US Letter, depending on where the file will be printed.

## PDF, Word or plain text?

Choose **PDF** when the text must look the same everywhere and should not be edited by accident. Choose **Word** when someone needs to edit it; the guide to [converting text to Word](/guides/how-to-convert-text-to-word/) shows how. Keep a plain **.txt** file when you only need the words, for instance to paste into another app. If your text uses Markdown symbols for headings and lists, see [how to convert Markdown to PDF](/guides/how-to-convert-markdown-to-pdf/) so the formatting is kept.

## Privacy and safety

If the text is private, check where the converter runs before pasting. A tool that processes text in your browser, such as the one linked above, never sends it away. Tools that "upload your file" work on someone else's server and may keep a copy. For contracts, medical or financial notes, always prefer a converter that runs locally, and remember that the PDF you save is an ordinary file on your device that you should delete when you no longer need it.
